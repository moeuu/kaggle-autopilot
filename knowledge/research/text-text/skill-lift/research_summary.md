## Ranked shortlist

### 1. Trajectory-optimized sparse progressive portfolio — recommended

**Leak-free features/encodings:** eight focused skills; root-level safety and exact-contract clauses; one-level optional references; deterministic inventory and artifact-validation scripts; task identifiers replaced by hashes; a frozen task-family/near-duplicate split; fold-fit word/character TF-IDF; frozen description and full-body embeddings; reranker margins; activation-count, collision, lint, provenance, and semantic supply-chain features. No oracle solution, verifier implementation, public answer, or final-holdout outcome may enter authoring. **Models and concrete hyperparameters:** deterministic compiler; optional `Qwen/Qwen3-8B-AWQ` patch proposer, batch 1, temperature 0.2, top-p 0.9, 1,536 new tokens; `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`; `ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`; logistic `C=1.0`, balanced class weight, 1,000 iterations; 3 grouped folds; 3 cheap screening seeds; 2 patches per iteration; ≤12 changed lines and ≤8% changed tokens; 12 paired development tasks; 20 frozen holdout tasks; one full paired seed/repeat; maximum 3 activated skills; hard zero-critical safety gate. **Expected runtime/memory:** 10–24 hours; 8–11.5 GB VRAM only while AWQ is loaded, otherwise 2–4 GB. **Leakage risk:** medium unless the split is frozen before edits and holdout trajectories are excluded. **Fallback:** deterministic failure-category templates, incumbent rollback, and lexical-only routing. SkillOpt’s bounded held-out editing and SkillsBench’s focused-skill results make this the best-aligned candidate. ([arXiv][2])

### 2. Scripted progressive static portfolio

**Leak-free features/encodings:** the same eight skills, safety roots, one-level references, scripts, fold-fit lexical features, frozen full-body embeddings, cross-encoder margin, per-skill calibration, synthetic near misses, safely labeled public-development instructions, and a blind activation gate. **Models and concrete hyperparameters:** MiniLM max length 512, embedding batch 24, rerank batch 4, 8 description variants per skill, 64 total candidates, rerank top 8, top-k 3, collision threshold 0.78, and blend weights 0.30 word / 0.20 char / 0.10 description dense / 0.30 body dense / 0.10 cross-encoder. **Expected runtime/memory:** 2–6 hours plus paired execution; 2–4 GB VRAM. **Leakage risk:** low-to-medium if public wording is never copied and routing remains auxiliary. **Fallback:** 0.60 word / 0.40 character TF-IDF with fold-fit thresholds. Full-body diagnostics remain enabled because body-resident signal can be decisive. ([arXiv][5])

### 3. Flat exact-contract portfolio

**Leak-free features/encodings:** six semantically matched self-contained roots, all mandatory constraints in immediate context, no references, identical scripts, the same activation/safety features, and a machine-readable semantic parity matrix. **Models and concrete hyperparameters:** deterministic compiler; 6 skills; root ≤4,200 tokens and ≤380 lines; top-k 2; collision threshold 0.78; 12 paired development tasks; 16 holdout tasks; one repeat; hard safety gate. **Expected runtime/memory:** under one hour to compile and screen; CPU or under 2 GB VRAM, with paired agents dominating. **Leakage risk:** low, but context-bloat and conflict risk are high. **Fallback:** promote only on exact-output, numeric, and long-artifact slices with no safety or domain regression. This is a controlled ablation because progressive disclosure is helpful on average but task-dependent. ([arXiv][3])

**Leak-free features/encodings:** one independently written broad plan–execute–verify skill, structural/frontmatter/archive checks, and provenance for `mpwolke/no-agent-skills-improvise-them-gemma4`. **Models and concrete hyperparameters:** deterministic one-skill generator; root ≤2,500 tokens and ≤220 lines; top-k 1; four paired control tasks. **Expected runtime/memory:** minutes, CPU-only. **Leakage risk:** low; over-activation and low-lift risk are very high. **Fallback:** never auto-promote; retain solely as reference reproduction, sanity check, and writeup control because the supplied notebook snapshot has no trustworthy paired score. 

The shortlist deliberately avoids heavy package installs. `numpy`, `pandas`, `scikit-learn`, `torch`, and `transformers` are preferred; missing dense models degrade to lexical screening, while missing local edit-model support degrades to deterministic bounded patches. None of these fallbacks may bypass matched validation or the hard safety gate.
